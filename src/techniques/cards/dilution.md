# Dilution and glazing

Making opaque paints translucent. It is what allows Slapchop without contrast paints and what smooths transitions in BSL.

## Ratios (paint : water or medium)
- **Base coat:** lightly thinned, like milk. Covers in about two coats.
- **Base over black:** usually needs three coats; the third one thinner (2:1).
- **Slapchop translucent colour:** start at 1:1 with medium and move towards 1:3 to see more of the sketch. Test on a piece of scrap plastic first.
- **Glaze:** like tinted water. Between 60 % and 90 % water depending on the paint, or 1:4 to 1:10 with medium. Go by how it looks.
- **Wash:** very thin (around 80 % thinner), so it pools in the recesses.
- Ratios are starting points: every paint behaves differently.

## How to glaze
- Load the brush and dab it off before touching the miniature: almost dry.
- Stroke in one direction, towards where you want the colour to gather. Scrubbing back and forth lifts the layer below.
- Let it dry between passes. Three to five soft glazes work better than one heavy one.

## Water or medium
- For light thinning, water is enough.
- For heavy thinning, water alone weakens the paint: it can dry chalky, leave tide marks or flake. Use medium, which thins the colour without breaking the paint film.
- If the paint beads up instead of spreading, it is too thin.
